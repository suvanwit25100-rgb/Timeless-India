What the archive saw — and what it did not

Thacker's Indian Directory listed 1 166 Calcutta firms in 1915. 513 were in White Town. Only 61 were on streets anyone today would place in Black Town. That ratio is not what the city looked like. It is what the colonial archive chose to look at.

Zoom in on Black Town

Chitpur Road was the spine of Black Town. The directory records none under that name — only variants that OCR'd to Chitpore. Kumartuli, home of the potter community, has no entries at all in the 1915 firm listing. Jorabagan has one.

What this means

The project does not try to pretend the colonial data covers the whole city. Where a listing exists, we read it carefully; where it does not, we say so in the map's sidebar. The Method page describes the human-in-the-loop verification that turns OCR noise into evidence, and the Ethics page sets out the consent and attribution rules for the fieldwork that will fill in the gap.

Some Black Town firms the archive does record — Biswas, Pulin Behari, plater & engraver, 104 Kansaripara Road; Nundo Lall Mullick & Bros, furniture, 40 Bowbazar Street, established 1863 — are among the most interesting rows in the whole file. They are small windows into a much larger business world the archive left unwritten.
